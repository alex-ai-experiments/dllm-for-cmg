Key Insights from dLLM Literature

EOS Overflow & Length Control

Instruction-tuned dLLMs suffer from <eos> overflow: as sequence length grows, outputs collapse into premature termination or padding. Rainbow Padding (7 distinct pad tokens, LoRA fine-tune for 1 epoch) fixes this cheaply. Separately, LR-DLLM decouples length from confidence for variable-length generation.

Rainbow Padding (arXiv:2510.03680) · LR-DLLM (arXiv:2602.07546)

AR → Diffusion Adaptation

DiffuLLaMA shows you can convert any AR model (GPT2, LLaMA 7B) into a diffusion model via continual pre-training on <200B tokens. The adapted models do in-context learning and fill-in-the-middle. This validates your Gemma4/Qwen2.5 conversion plan.

DiffuLLaMA (arXiv:2410.17891, ICLR 2025)

Code-Specific Diffusion

DiffuCoder (7B, 130B code tokens) reveals that dLLMs dynamically decide generation order — no fixed left-to-right. Temperature diversifies both token choice and generation order, creating rich RL search spaces. Stable-DiffCoder shows block diffusion CPT outperforms AR under same budget.

DiffuCoder (arXiv:2506.20639) · Stable-DiffCoder (arXiv:2601.15892)

Quantization for dLLMs

First systematic PTQ study for dLLMs finds activation outliers are the key bottleneck. W8A8 works reasonably; W4 needs careful outlier-aware methods. Different tasks degrade differently — generation quality is more sensitive than understanding tasks.

Quantization Meets dLLMs (arXiv:2508.14896)

Diffusion for Summarization

Discrete diffusion with semantic-aware noising achieves SOTA on Gigaword/CNN/DailyMail summarization while being faster at inference than AR. This directly validates your dLLM-as-summarizer pipeline idea.

DiffuSum (arXiv:2407.10998, NAACL 2025)

DiffusionBERT & BERT-based dLLMs

BERT can serve as initialization for discrete diffusion (absorbing state) with custom noise schedules. Token-level information-based noise scheduling significantly improves generation quality. Directly applicable to CommitBERT conversion.

DiffusionBERT (arXiv:2211.15029, ACL 2023)

Proposed Research Questions

RQ1: How do discrete diffusion language models compare to autoregressive baselines for commit message generation across quality metrics, throughput, and output length control?

Grounded in your existing 66-config ablation. Addresses the core question: are dLLMs viable for CMG? Your data already shows dLLMs beat AR on METEOR/CIDEr but generate 2–4× more tokens, and lose on ROUGE-L/BLEU.

RQ2: Can AR-to-diffusion model adaptation and domain-specific fine-tuning close the quality gap between general-purpose dLLMs and task-specialized AR models for commit message generation?

Tests converting Qwen 2.5 Coder, Gemma 4, and CommitBERT into dLLMs + fine-tuning on your CMG training data. Measures whether domain knowledge (code understanding) transfers through the diffusion adaptation process.

RQ3: What is the effect of inference-time optimization techniques (KV caching, speculative decoding, quantization, length regularization) on dLLM efficiency for structured short-text generation?

Investigates the practical deployment angle: quantized LLaDA 2 mini, Fast-dLLM speedups, Rainbow Padding for length control. Commit messages are short (typically <72 chars for subject), making length control especially important.

RQ4: Can a two-stage pipeline (dLLM code summarizer → LLM commit message generator) outperform end-to-end approaches by reducing context length and leveraging the parallel decoding strengths of diffusion models?

Your novel architectural contribution. dLLMs can process the full diff in parallel and produce a compact summary; the LLM then generates the final commit message from a much shorter context. Tests whether decomposition beats monolithic generation.

RQ5: How do prompt engineering and in-context learning strategies differ in effectiveness between diffusion and autoregressive language models for commit message generation?

dLLMs process the full prompt bidirectionally, so prompt design may have qualitatively different effects. Tests few-shot examples, structured prompts, and template-based in-context learning.

Evaluation of Proposed Research Directions

1. Fix dLLM Over-Generation (more tokens than AR)
Strong idea. Your data shows dLLMs generate 40–178 tokens vs AR's 16.6. This is a known problem: dLLMs lack a native stopping mechanism and fill the entire block. Rainbow Padding (LoRA fine-tune, 1 epoch, 7 pad tokens) and LR-DLLM (length-regularized inference) are proven solutions. For CMG specifically, you can also constrain max_new_tokens to 64 (your data shows mnt=64 already reaches near-peak quality at METEOR=0.103) and use ρ-EOS for variable-length control. This is a must-do before any other experiments — it's the biggest confound in your current results.
Essential & Feasible
2. Convert Gemma 4, Qwen 2.5 Coder, CommitBERT to dLLMs
Good idea with precedent. DiffuLLaMA (ICLR 2025) demonstrates AR→diffusion conversion for models up to 7B parameters. Qwen 2.5 Coder is the strongest candidate — it already understands code, and conversion should preserve that knowledge (DiffuLLaMA showed skill transfer). Gemma 4 is interesting but likely too large for your GPU budget in 6 weeks. Consider the 2B variant. CommitBERT → DiffusionBERT-style conversion is novel for CMG and low-cost (encoder-only, small model). However, generating commit messages from a BERT-based model requires careful seq2seq architecture design. Risk: Training compute. Focus on 1–2 models maximum.
High Impact
3. Quantize LLaDA 2 Mini as a General dLLM
Feasible and well-timed. The "Quantization Meets dLLMs" paper provides a systematic framework. LLaDA 2 mini is small enough for PTQ experimentation. W8A8 quantization typically preserves 95%+ quality; W4 is riskier for generation tasks. Caveat: LLaDA isn't code-specialized, so for CMG the quantized model may still underperform a code-tuned dLLM. This is best positioned as a deployment efficiency contribution rather than a quality improvement.
Useful but Secondary
4. Fine-tune a dLLM for CMG Using Task Builder Data
Excellent idea — core contribution territory. Fine-tuning dLLMs is underexplored for domain-specific tasks. Your training data pipeline is ready. LLaDA 1.5 introduced variance-reduced preference optimization for dLLMs; simpler SFT + LoRA should work for CMG. Blockwise SFT (arXiv:2508.19529) shows how to reconcile bidirectional attention with AR-like fine-tuning for block diffusion. This directly answers RQ2 and provides the strongest potential for publication — no one has fine-tuned a dLLM for commit message generation.
Core Contribution
5. Prompt Engineering & In-Context Learning for dLLMs
Low-cost, high-signal experiment. DiffuLLaMA showed dLLMs can do ICL. But dLLMs process prompts bidirectionally, so prompt position/structure may matter differently than in AR models. "Thinking Inside the Mask" (arXiv:2508.10736) explores in-place prompting specifically for diffusion LLMs. Warning: Your current model (Fast-dLLM v2 1.5B) may be too small for strong ICL. Larger models (3B+) show much better few-shot performance. This is a cheap experiment that can be done in parallel with training runs.
Quick Win
6. Two-Stage Pipeline: dLLM Summarizer → LLM Generator
Novel and well-motivated. DiffuSum proves dLLMs work for summarization. The key insight: dLLMs can process long diffs in parallel (no sequential bottleneck), producing a compact "code change summary" that an LLM then converts to a commit message. This addresses the context length problem elegantly and leverages dLLM's strength (parallel processing of full context) while avoiding its weakness (precise short-text generation). Risk: Error propagation — a bad summary produces a bad commit message. Needs careful evaluation of intermediate summaries. SPREAD (arXiv:2601.11342) shows RAG-augmented dLLMs suffer from "Response Semantic Drift" — the summary may drift from the original diff semantics. Consider their query-relevance-guided denoising.
Novel Contribution
